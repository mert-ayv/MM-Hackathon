# Playbook: RFQ (2-3 bidders quote for one part; we award one)

**The lever is competition.** Every bidder knows it is not alone; the cheapest rival offer is the
strongest argument we have. Cite it (its `offer:` ID, a verified argument) only to bidders that are
dearer than it - citing it reveals the figure.

**Anchor below the field, not below this bidder.** The rules open 22 % below a bidder's price and at
least 8 % below the cheapest rival's. Suppliers converge on our anchor: in P05 (three founder-led job
shops) a hard opening ended at EUR 28.13 (score 0.81), a soft one at EUR 33.00 (0.35). The house agent
scored 0.56 there.

**"Agreed" at once means room left.** A bidder that agrees to our first or second ask took it as it
stood. While another bidder holds a valid offer as backup, ask it once more, lower.

**Keep two bidders alive** until the leader stops moving. Awarding early throws the competition away.

**Compare total cost, not price.** Freight (EXW/FCA cost us per unit, DAP does not), lead time beyond
our need (a cost per day), payment days (8 % a year) and coverage all count. A cheaper EXW offer can
lose to a dearer DAP one - price_package shows it.

**Cash-short bidders trade price for payment.** Founder-led job shops often need cash: in P05 Hrvelec
asked EUR 35.00 at 90 days but 31.00 at 30 days - over 10 % for 60 days that cost us about 1.3 %. Offer
14-30 days in an alternative sheet when the profile or its e-mails show cash pressure.

**Lead time is often a must-have** (e.g. at most 42 days). Never propose or accept a sheet that breaks it.
