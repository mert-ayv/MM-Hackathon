# RFQ: competition and complete packages

Compare bidders using complete packages, standard terms and must-haves. A lower unit price can lose
on freight, delivery, quality, tooling or coverage. Use current rival evidence, never invent a rival.
Revealing a rival's offer is a strategic decision: check that it actually improves on the recipient's.

The rfq_competition table ranks valid first standing sheets by estimated total cost. Review invalid
bids separately: a cheap quote that misses the delivery must-have cannot anchor an award. Alternatives
need confirmation and are not yet secured competition. Use compare_rfq_bids with your proposed terms
to calculate the unit price needed to match a valid rival, including this bidder's own quality and
standard terms. This target is a comparison, not an instruction to chase an uneconomic price.

Discover what each bidder values and test conditional trades. Cash, lead time, contract length and
volume have different values for different suppliers; avoid category stereotypes without evidence.

Keeping another valid offer can make further bargaining less risky, but an early agreement is not
proof that more concessions are available. Choose when to award based on all live offers, supplier
responses and time, rather than a fixed anchor percentage or number of rounds.

Keep a separate hypothesis for each bidder: its binding constraint, useful trade and next question.
Offer conditional payment or delivery packages when their value can overcome the cost gap. Do not
promise more volume, a split award or tooling terms when the enquiry fixes them. A supplier asking
for faster payment does not prove that it will discount enough to compensate us; price the exchange.

Stopping this thread preserves its current offer for coordinator comparison. RFQ accept/walk_away
requests do not close the auction directly; the coordinator checks all current valid bids at closing.

Only the first standing sheet can be accepted. Propose an attractive alternative sheet back unchanged
and obtain confirmation before accepting it. Never let an attractive price conceal a must-have failure.
